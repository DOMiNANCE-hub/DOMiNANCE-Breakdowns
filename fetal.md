# Fetal Circulation and the Transition at Birth

## Why fetal flow takes shortcuts
Before birth, gas exchange happens in the **placenta**, not the fluid-filled lungs. Oxygenated blood returns through the **umbilical vein**. Some passes through the liver, while a substantial fraction bypasses hepatic sinusoids through the **ductus venosus** to the inferior vena cava.

Because fetal pulmonary vascular resistance is high, right-heart flow partly bypasses lungs by two pathways:
- **Foramen ovale:** right atrium → left atrium (favoured by fetal pressure relations and directed IVC flow).
- **Ductus arteriosus:** pulmonary trunk → descending aorta.

Two **umbilical arteries** carry fetal blood to the placenta. Oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor streams mix, so fetal saturation varies between vessels and chambers.

## What changes after the first breaths?
Lung expansion and increasing alveolar oxygen lower pulmonary vascular resistance, increasing pulmonary blood flow and left atrial return. Umbilical cord clamping removes placental low-resistance flow and increases systemic vascular resistance. Rising **left atrial pressure relative to right** functionally closes the foramen ovale; increased oxygen and reduced prostaglandin influence constrict the ductus arteriosus.

## Fetal haemoglobin
**HbF** has higher oxygen affinity than adult HbA, in part because it binds **2,3-BPG** less strongly. This helps fetal blood acquire oxygen across the placenta. HbF declines after birth as adult haemoglobin replaces it over months.

## Trace the preferentially oxygenated fetal stream
The placenta oxygenates fetal blood. The **umbilical vein** brings comparatively oxygen-rich blood toward the liver; some flows through the **ductus venosus** to the IVC. Preferential streaming across the **foramen ovale** supplies left atrium → left ventricle → ascending aorta, supporting perfusion of the developing heart and brain. Much right ventricular output enters pulmonary trunk and passes through **ductus arteriosus** into descending aorta, bypassing the high-resistance unventilated lungs. **Umbilical arteries** return lower-oxygen blood to placenta.

## What closes after delivery?
First breaths expand lungs and increase alveolar oxygen, sharply reducing **pulmonary vascular resistance** and increasing pulmonary venous return. Cord clamping removes the low-resistance placental bed and increases **systemic vascular resistance**. Higher left than right atrial pressure functionally closes the foramen ovale, while rising oxygen and lower prostaglandin signalling promote **ductus arteriosus constriction**. Anatomical closure/remodelling follows functional closure on a different time scale; persistent patency is clinically relevant.

**HbF** holds oxygen more avidly than HbA partly due to lower 2,3-BPG affinity. HbF does not invariably disappear by an exact fourth postnatal month; replacement is gradual over months.

**Practice:** Why does pulmonary blood flow rise after birth even as systemic resistance increases? Lung expansion/oxygen directly reduce **pulmonary** resistance; placental separation independently raises **systemic** resistance.

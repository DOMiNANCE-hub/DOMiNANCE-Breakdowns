# Fetal Circulation and the Transition at Birth

## 1. Why does the fetus need shortcuts?

Before birth, the **placenta**, not the lungs, does gas exchange. The fetal lungs are filled with fluid, and their vessels are tightly constricted, so **pulmonary resistance is very high**. Pumping all the blood through them would be wasted effort.

So the fetal circulation has **three shortcuts (shunts)** that send oxygen-rich blood where it is needed, mainly the heart and brain, and route most of it **past the liver and lungs**.

## 2. Trace the fetal circuit

1. **<span data-term="placenta">Placenta</span> → umbilical vein:** the most oxygen-rich blood in the fetus (yet still only about 80% saturated) travels up the **single umbilical vein**.
2. **Shortcut 1: ductus venosus.** Much of it **bypasses the liver**, going straight to the inferior vena cava.
3. **Into the right atrium.** The stream from the IVC is aimed straight across the atrium at an opening in the septum.
4. **Shortcut 2: foramen ovale.** This oxygen-rich stream crosses **from the right atrium to the left atrium**, then goes to the left ventricle → aorta → **heart and brain first**.
5. **SVC blood** (oxygen-poor, from the head) goes mainly into the right ventricle → pulmonary trunk.
6. **Shortcut 3: ductus arteriosus.** Pulmonary resistance is high, so most of this blood **bypasses the lungs**, crossing **from the pulmonary trunk into the descending aorta**.
7. **Back to the placenta:** the **two umbilical arteries** (branches of the internal iliac arteries) carry blood back to be reoxygenated.

| Shunt | Connects | Bypasses |
|---|---|---|
| **Ductus venosus** | Umbilical vein → inferior vena cava | The liver |
| **Foramen ovale** | Right atrium → left atrium | The lungs (via the right ventricle) |
| **Ductus arteriosus** | Pulmonary trunk → aorta | The lungs |

**Why does blood flow right-to-left through the shunts?** Because, before birth, the **right side** has the higher pressure: pulmonary resistance is high, while the placenta keeps systemic resistance low.

## 3. Fetal haemoglobin: grabbing oxygen from the mother

**Fetal haemoglobin (HbF)** holds oxygen **more tightly** than adult haemoglobin (HbA), because it binds the molecule **2,3-BPG** less strongly. 2,3-BPG normally loosens haemoglobin's grip on oxygen. So, at the low oxygen levels in the placenta, fetal blood can **pull oxygen across from the mother's blood**. After birth, HbF is gradually replaced by HbA over several months.

## 4. What closes after delivery?

Two events at birth flip the pressures:

1. **The first breaths:** the lungs fill with air and oxygen rises, so the lung vessels **dilate** and **pulmonary resistance falls sharply**. Lung blood flow surges, and much more blood returns to the **left atrium**.
2. **Clamping the cord:** the placenta, a **low-resistance** circuit, is cut off, so **systemic resistance rises**. The flow up the umbilical vein stops.

Now **the left side has the higher pressure**, and each shunt closes:

| Shunt | Why it closes | Becomes (anatomical remnant) |
|---|---|---|
| **Foramen ovale** | Left atrial pressure now **exceeds** right, which presses the flap shut | **Fossa ovalis** |
| **Ductus arteriosus** | **Higher oxygen** and **less prostaglandin** (from the placenta) make it constrict | **Ligamentum arteriosum** |
| **Ductus venosus** | No more umbilical venous flow | **Ligamentum venosum** |
| Umbilical vein | No more flow | **Ligamentum teres** (of the liver) |
| Umbilical arteries | No more flow | **Medial umbilical ligaments** |

**Closure happens in two steps:** **functional** closure within minutes to days, as flow stops; then **anatomical** closure over weeks to months, as tissue seals the channel.

## 5. Chapter Essentials

1. **The placenta** does fetal gas exchange. The fluid-filled lungs have high resistance.
2. **Three shunts:** ductus venosus (bypasses the liver), foramen ovale (right → left atrium), ductus arteriosus (pulmonary trunk → aorta).
3. **The best-oxygenated blood** goes through the foramen ovale to the heart and brain.
4. **Before birth the right side has the higher pressure**, so flow through the shunts is right-to-left.
5. **HbF** binds oxygen more tightly (less 2,3-BPG binding), so it loads oxygen from the mother.
6. **At birth:** the first breaths lower pulmonary resistance; cord clamping raises systemic resistance; **left pressure > right**.
7. **Remnants:** foramen ovale → fossa ovalis; ductus arteriosus → ligamentum arteriosum; ductus venosus → ligamentum venosum; umbilical vein → ligamentum teres; umbilical arteries → medial umbilical ligaments.

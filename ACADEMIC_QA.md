# Academic QA — do not reproduce inaccurate lecture statements uncritically

These items are **quality-review flags**, not claims that the lecturer is necessarily testing an incorrect answer. Keep lecturer terminology visible while explaining the best-supported physiology accurately.

1. **Cardiac cycle**: ventricular ejection is normally initially **rapid**, then reduced; slide 15 of Circulation 1 appears to reverse the sequence. AV-valve closure is caused by rising ventricular pressure rather than initiating ventricular contraction.
2. **Laplace law**: Circulation 2 p10 contains inconsistent expressions. For a thin-walled cylindrical vessel, circumferential wall tension per unit length approximates transmural pressure × radius; spherical forms differ by a factor of two. Do not copy the incorrect summation denominator.
3. **Preload versus afterload**: Frank-Starling principle primarily describes dependence on initial muscle fibre length (preload); afterload is a separate determinant of ejection. The source links both ambiguously in Circulation 1 p38.
4. **Coronary beta-1 effect**: Regional Circulation p28 says increased cardiac beta-1 stimulation produces metabolites that cause coronary constriction. Increased myocardial metabolism generally causes local coronary *vasodilatation*; direct alpha-mediated vasoconstriction can be opposed by metabolic vasodilatation.
5. **Capillary Starling forces**: Circulation 1 pp85-89 uses traditional filtration and routine venous-end reabsorption; contemporary glycocalyx model emphasizes net filtration in many continuous capillaries and lymphatic return; sustained venous reabsorption is limited under usual steady-state conditions.
6. **U wave origin**: ECG slide p10 assigns a definitive source. The exact genesis of U waves remains debated; avoid categorical assertion.
7. **ECG QT**: QT varies with heart rate. Explain QTc and formula limitations; the slide's 0.2-0.4s is not a universal normal value.
8. **Cushing reflex**: Raised intracranial pressure may lower cerebral perfusion pressure and cause sympathetic hypertension followed by baroreflex bradycardia. Avoid suggesting the increase in BP is produced directly by baroreceptor stimulation.
9. **Heart rate vs BP**: Regulation slide p33 suggests HR↑ invariably raises BP. HR alone does not determine BP: MAP depends on cardiac output and total peripheral resistance, and stroke volume may decrease in marked tachycardia.
10. **Postural hypotension**: Orthostatic hypotension more often features compensatory tachycardia if autonomic reflexes are intact; bradycardia suggests vagal mechanisms or an atypical context.
11. **Blood loss Hb/Hct**: Whole-blood hemorrhage initially removes red cells and plasma together, so concentration may change little immediately; later fluid redistribution/resuscitation lowers measured Hb/Hct.
12. **Meninges/subdural space**: The subarachnoid space normally contains CSF; normal subdural space is only potential, not a second CSF-filled compartment (Regional Circulation p5).
13. **Skeletal versus cardiac tetanus**: prolonged cardiac refractory period overlaps contraction; this is an educational addition not fully described in slide set.
14. **Copyright/images**: lecture slides include third-party diagrams and photographs. For a public site, use independently produced or licensed diagrams; do not publish entire slide image collections without permission.

For every topic, retain source filename/page range, distinguish lectures from supplementary material, and confirm all figures/equations before public launch.

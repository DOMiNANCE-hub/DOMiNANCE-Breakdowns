# Cerebral Circulation, CSF and the Blood–Brain Barrier

## Supply and flow regulation
The **internal carotid** and **vertebrobasilar** systems supply the brain and connect through arteries of the **Circle of Willis**, offering potential collateral routes; the circle is variable and does not guarantee protection from stroke. Cerebral perfusion depends on driving pressure and vascular resistance. **Cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP) ≈ MAP − ICP** when intracranial pressure exceeds venous outflow pressure.

Cerebral vessels autoregulate over a range of pressures. Increased arterial CO₂ is a potent cerebral vasodilator; marked hypocapnia constricts cerebral arterioles. Severe hypoxia can also cause vasodilatation. Blood flow is adjusted regionally to neural activity.

## Intracranial pressure and the Monro–Kellie principle
Within the largely fixed adult skull, brain tissue, blood and CSF share a limited volume. Added volume can initially be offset by shifting CSF/venous blood, but beyond compensation **ICP rises sharply**, potentially reducing CPP and causing the Cushing response.

## CSF path and functions
CSF is produced mainly by **choroid plexus**, flows from lateral ventricles through interventricular foramina (Monro) to third ventricle → cerebral aqueduct → fourth ventricle → median/lateral apertures → subarachnoid space, and returns ultimately to venous/lymphatic drainage pathways. Typical adult CSF volume is about **150 mL**. It provides buoyancy and cushioning and supports CNS waste/solute handling.

**Hydrocephalus** may result from impaired flow or absorption. The subarachnoid space normally contains CSF; the subdural space is a **potential**, not normally CSF-filled, space.

## Blood–brain barrier (BBB)
Specialised brain capillary **endothelial tight junctions**, transporters and neurovascular unit limit uncontrolled solute passage. Glucose enters through transporters (not simply free diffusion), while many polar compounds require specialised transport. BBB integrity is important for neuronal extracellular stability and drug access.

## Identify the arterial supplies and vulnerability
The **anterior, middle and posterior cerebral arteries** supply different territories. The two internal carotids and vertebrobasilar system connect variably in the **Circle of Willis**, potentially supporting collateral supply; the middle cerebral artery is a large terminal branch but **not itself part of the arterial circle**. The actual territories and clinical consequences of occlusion vary; the lecturer includes **ischaemic** (thrombotic/embolic) and **haemorrhagic** stroke as broad categories.

## Meninges, CSF and pressure
From outside inward: **dura mater**, **arachnoid mater**, **subarachnoid space containing CSF**, then **pia mater** adhering to brain/spinal cord. The normal subdural space is only potential. CSF is formed mainly in the ventricular choroid plexuses; normal adult total volume is about **150 mL**, with production several hundred mL/day. Flow proceeds lateral ventricles → **foramina of Monro** → third ventricle → **aqueduct of Sylvius** → fourth ventricle → median (Magendie) and lateral (Luschka) apertures → subarachnoid space. CSF ultimately leaves through arachnoid and lymphatic outflow pathways. **Communicating hydrocephalus** generally has impaired CSF handling beyond the ventricular pathway; **noncommunicating hydrocephalus** has an obstruction within it.

CSF protects via **buoyancy and cushioning** and supports interstitial solute clearance. **Lumbar puncture** samples subarachnoid CSF below the usual adult spinal cord termination; do not infer puncture is risk-free when raised ICP or mass effect is suspected.

## Cerebral blood flow control
**CPP ≈ MAP − ICP** in the common approximation, and high ICP compresses intracranial vessels. Local vessels respond strongly to **CO₂**: rising CO₂ usually dilates and falling CO₂ constricts cerebral arterioles. Brain tissue normally relies heavily on continuous glucose/oxygen delivery, using glucose transport across the BBB and alternative fuels such as **ketone bodies during prolonged starvation**; the slides' suggestion of freely substituting fatty acids is not a general statement of normal brain metabolism.

The **BBB** depends on continuous nonfenestrated endothelial cells with tight junctions, specialised transporters and associated cells. Glucose uses **GLUT1**, and lipid-soluble gases diffuse comparatively readily. The BBB is **not** the same as the blood–CSF barrier at the choroid plexus.

**Practice:** A brain tumour increases cranial volume beyond compensatory shifts. ICP rises, CPP can fall, and brain perfusion may worsen even if peripheral BP is unchanged.

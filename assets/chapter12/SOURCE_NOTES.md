- 97–98: low venous pressure towards the right atrium, respiratory pump, skeletal-muscle pump, venous pooling, intracranial dural sinuses and air embolism.

## Supplementary explanation
- VR equals CO in steady state. Conceptual Guyton relation VR = (Pmsf − Pra)/Rvr, with limitations when thoracic veins collapse.
- The lecture's absolute venous pressures (including 4.6 mmHg near right atrium) are context-dependent, not universally normative.
- Venoconstriction can increase stressed blood volume and return without changing total blood volume.
- The reason standing soldiers contract their calf muscles is prevention of dependent venous pooling; valves favour upward flow.
- A dural sinus does not collapse readily because of attachment to dura, creating air entry risk when pressure becomes subatmospheric.

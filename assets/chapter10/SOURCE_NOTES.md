## Supplementary clarifications
- The MAP one-third rule is an approximation depending on heart rate and waveform shape.
- Arm below heart level yields positive hydrostatic bias; above heart yields negative bias. Support at heart level and avoid isometric effort.
- A cuff too small generally overestimates BP, while one too large can underestimate.
- Korotkoff **Phase I**: first repeated tapping sound = systolic; **Phase V**: sound disappearance = adult diastolic, with clinical exceptions.
- Palpation can estimate systolic, not reliably diastolic pressure.
- Proper seating, rest, arm support, deflation rate and repeated readings help standardise measurement.
- Maintained pressure in haemorrhagic shock does not demonstrate adequate tissue perfusion.
- Blood-pressure classification thresholds should be applied using appropriate clinical guidance, not the lecturer's single illustrative 120/70 value.

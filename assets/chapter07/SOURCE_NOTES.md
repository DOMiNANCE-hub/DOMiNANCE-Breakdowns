## Clarifications separated from lecture wording
- The downward-arrow note about exercise and venous return on slide 49 is ambiguous/inconsistent with the muscle-pump diagrams in slides 50–51; typical dynamic exercise increases venous return.
- The vagus acts strongly on HR and AV conduction; its direct negative inotropic effect on ventricular myocardium is relatively weak.
- Most ventricular β1 stimulation arises from sympathetic nerve noradrenaline, with circulating adrenaline chiefly from adrenal medulla. Some wording in slide 45 reverses the usual source assignments.
- EDV and filling pressure are **surrogates**, not perfect interchangeable measures, of myocardial preload.
- Afterload is not identical to systemic vascular resistance; pressure and wall stress matter.
- The extreme descending Frank–Starling curve shown on slide 39 is not a normal operating state of the intact human heart.
- Curve shifts can result from inotropic changes or changes in afterload, so they are not a unique test of contractility.
- Everyday caffeine use should not be presented as guaranteeing large positive inotropy.

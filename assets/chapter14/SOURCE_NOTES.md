# Chapter 14 — Formation of Oedema
**Lecture:** CIRCULATION 1(4).pdf slides 89–95.

## Lecture coverage
- Capillary permeability mediators and Starling filtration.
- Oedema from raised capillary/venous hydrostatic pressure (heart failure, venous obstruction, pregnancy, standing).
- Reduced plasma oncotic pressure from liver disease, renal losses and malnutrition.
- Inflammation, lymphatic insufficiency, filariasis and post-mastectomy lymphoedema.

## Supplementary clarifications
- Traditional Jv = Kf[(Pc − Pi) − σ(πc − πi)] is used to explain forces; the glycocalyx alters the effective oncotic gradient.
- The 85% venous-end reabsorption rule is the classic lecture explanation, not universal steady-state physiology.
- Lymphatic obstruction impedes interstitial fluid and protein clearance; early lymphoedema may pit, while chronic fibrosis often produces non-pitting swelling.
- Renal sodium/water retention may amplify fluid overload even where the initiating mechanism differs.
- The goal is to identify the cause of excess tissue fluid, not infer one unique diagnosis from oedema pattern alone.

# Chapter 15 — Lymphatic Circulation
**Lecture:** CIRCULATION 1(4).pdf slides 60 and 94–95, with Regional Circulation Proper.pdf slides 47–48 showing lymphatic entry and drainage anatomy.

## Lecture coverage
- Lymph vessel valves, contractile walls, skeletal-muscle and respiratory pumping.
- Return of tissue fluid/proteins to the venous system near subclavian veins.
- Intestinal lipid transport, filarial elephantiasis and lymphatic disruption with radical mastectomy.
- Regional Circulation illustrations depict tissue lymph entry and main ducts.

## Supplementary precision
- Thoracic duct drains most of body, right lymphatic duct generally the right upper quadrant; opening usually near venous angles.
- Lacteals absorb long-chain lipid-rich chylomicrons formed by intestinal enterocytes; short/medium-chain fatty acids may instead enter portal blood.
- Lymph nodes support immune surveillance; collecting lymphatics and external pumps supply much of lymph propulsion.
- Early lymphoedema may pit; late fibrotic lymphoedema frequently becomes non-pitting.
- The lecture implies lymphatics affect renal water balance; the direct mechanism is interstitial fluid return, not performance of renal tubular water reabsorption.

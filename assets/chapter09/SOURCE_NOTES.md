## Corrections/qualification
- Reynolds number is written **Re**; vascular resistance is written **R** to avoid the lecture's shorthand ambiguity.
- The slide 58 gaps/fenestrations cannot be measured in millimetres as printed. Specific pore sizes differ with microvascular anatomy and are covered later.
- Capillary sphincters are not universal individual caps around every capillary, and local vascular control differs among organs.
- Capillary velocity depends on the enormous *total cross-sectional area* of many capillaries, not the size of each capillary alone.
- Poiseuille's formula assumes laminar, steady, rigid-tube flow of a Newtonian fluid. Blood and living vessels violate some assumptions, so the radius-fourth changes are ideal predictions.
- Narrowing may increase local velocity but reduce overall volume flow; the two must not be conflated.
- Viscosity effects depend on haematocrit, vessel scale and disease, not a universal 'minor' contribution.
- Slide 75 blood-volume percentages are approximate and do not constitute a complete exact 100% partition.

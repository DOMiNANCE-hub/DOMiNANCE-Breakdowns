# Chapter 13 — Microcirculation and Capillary Exchange
**Lecture:** CIRCULATION 1(4).pdf slides 57–60 and 84–89.

## Lecture coverage
- Microvascular structure, capillaries and exchange types, local metarteriolar/precapillary control.
- Capillary fluid transport: diffusion, filtration, fenestrations and vesicular routes.
- Starling's hydrostatic and colloid osmotic forces (slides 85–88).
- Permeability mediators histamine, bradykinin, substance P (slide 89).

## Essential scientific distinctions
- Slide 58 labels endothelial openings with 'mm', an untenable unit: fenestrations are usually nanometre-scale.
- Basal lamina does not require a thick subendothelial connective-tissue layer.
- Capillary sphincters in classical illustrations do not mean that every tissue has an identical sphincter at each capillary.
- The lecture's 24 L/day, 85% reabsorption and 15% lymphatic example is the classic model. It is labelled as such, not silently overwritten.
- The revised Starling principle identifies endothelial glycocalyx and subglycocalyx gradients; steady-state net filtration may persist even in venular microvessels, with lymphatics responsible for fluid return.

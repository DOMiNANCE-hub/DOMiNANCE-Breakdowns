# Chapter 11 — Arterial and Jugular Venous Pulses
**Lecture:** CIRCULATION 1(4).pdf slides 22–26.

## Lecture coverage
- Slides 22–23: arterial pulse is a travelling pressure/distension wave; speed relates to arterial stiffness; rate, volume, strength, character and location.
- Slides 24–25: internal jugular connection to the right atrium; JVP falls on inspiration; waves a, c, v and descents x, y.
- Slide 26: JVP examination around 45 degrees (adjustable), congestion, tricuspid incompetence and large atrial waves in AV dissociation.

## Explicit clarifications
- Arterial pulse-wave velocity is not the mean forward velocity of blood.
- JVP a is atrial contraction, c includes tricuspid bulging/arterial transmission, v is filling with tricuspid closed, x atrial relaxation, y tricuspid opening and filling.
- The slide calls tricuspid incompetence a 'big c wave'; clinical descriptions more commonly identify prominent v or c–v waves from systolic regurgitation.
- Cannon a waves result from atrial contraction against a closed tricuspid valve; these are distinct from the large v waves of tricuspid regurgitation.
- JVP estimation angle should be adjusted to bring the top of the venous column into view, not forced to 45 degrees for every patient.

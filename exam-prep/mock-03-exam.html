01. An ECG marker is placed at the beginning of the P wave. Where must the second marker go to measure the PR interval?

A. At the beginning of the T wave.

B. At the beginning of the QRS complex.

C. At the end of the ventricular QRS complex.

D. At the end of the ventricular T wave.

02. Which response becomes particularly important for supporting pressure during sustained rather than momentary upright posture?

A. Renin–angiotensin effects and renal salt retention.

B. Suppression of aldosterone-driven sodium retention.

C. Persistent inhibition of all vascular reflexes.

D. Loss of sympathetic arteriolar responsiveness.

03. A person becomes angry during an argument, and both pulse and pressure rise. Which route can initiate this response?

A. Cortical signals acting through the hypothalamus on autonomic centres.

B. Atrial filling signals acting through the kidneys on increased urine output.

C. Cerebral CSF signals acting through the ventricles on the heart.

D. Platelet serotonin acting through capillaries on cardiac pacing.

04. Why can vision fail before consciousness during strong positive acceleration?

A. Retinal perfusion falls as blood shifts toward the feet.

B. The retina receives blood at higher pressure than the heart.

C. Intracranial pressure exceeds local arterial pressure.

D. Positive acceleration shifts blood toward the head.

05. Which combination correctly characterises isovolumetric ventricular contraction?

A. All valves closed, ventricular pressure rises, volume fixed.

B. AV valves open, semilunar valves open, volume rises.

C. AV valves open, semilunar valves shut, volume falls.

D. All valves closed, ventricular pressure falls, volume fixed.

06. A patient loses substantial albumin into urine during kidney disease. What Starling-force change is expected?

A. Increased red-cell viscosity causing filtration.

B. Reduced capillary permeability everywhere.

C. Increased plasma colloid osmotic pressure.

D. Reduced plasma oncotic pressure promotes oedema.

07. Why is perfusion of the left ventricular myocardium predominantly diastolic?

A. Ventricular contraction compresses intramural vessels.

B. The coronary ostia close during ventricular systole.

C. Aortic flow stops entirely during ventricular diastole.

D. The left ventricle receives no blood during relaxation.

08. Beta-1 adrenergic stimulation generally increases ventricular contractility through which sequence?

A. Blocked ryanodine receptors → larger systolic calcium release.

B. Increased cAMP/PKA signalling → greater calcium availability.

C. Increased vagal tone → reduced pacemaker slope → stronger contraction.

D. Reduced cAMP → less calcium entry → weaker contraction.

09. A person lying still stands up, then begins walking. What feature makes the calf-muscle pump effective?

A. Venule dilation without pressure gradients.

B. Capillary contraction during each footstep.

C. Deep vein compression with one-way valves.

D. Arterial compression with bidirectional valves.

10. Why may arterial blood pressure rise sharply during severe medullary ischaemia caused by raised intracranial pressure?

A. Direct vasomotor-centre inhibition greatly decreases total sympathetic outflow.

B. Increased vagal discharge is the main cause of the pressure rise.

C. Direct vasomotor-centre excitation greatly increases sympathetic outflow.

D. Increased atrial ANP release is the main cause of the pressure rise.

11. A standard ECG uses ten skin electrodes but produces twelve electrical views. How is this possible?

A. Two extra electrodes inside the heart are automatically activated for recording.

B. Several leads are calculated from combinations of the same electrode voltages.

C. Each electrode produces one lead, with two more produced by blood-pressure sensors.

D. The limb electrodes are relocated to the chest during every cardiac cycle.

12. Why does positioning an accelerating pilot more horizontally improve tolerance to positive g?

A. It shortens the heart-to-brain hydrostatic column.

B. It increases the distance between the heart and brain.

C. It directs pooled blood toward the ankles.

D. It raises the capillary filtration rate in the legs.

13. A ventricular depolarisation vector runs almost perpendicular to the viewing axis of a lead. What should that lead record?

A. A small or biphasic ventricular deflection.

B. A tall downward ventricular deflection.

C. A tall upright ventricular deflection.

D. A prolonged ventricular depolarisation interval.

14. A vascular bed has a pressure difference of 80 mmHg and resistance of 20 mmHg·min/L. What is its flow?

A. 2 L/min.

B. 4 L/min.

C. 8 L/min.

D. 3 L/min.

15. In complete AV dissociation, the right atrium occasionally contracts against a closed tricuspid valve. What JVP abnormality may follow?

A. An isolated giant v wave after every beat.

B. Intermittent cannon a waves.

C. A steady nonpulsatile jugular column.

D. Loss of all visible venous pulsations.

16. During standing, head-level arterial pressure falls by 30 mmHg and cerebral venous pressure falls by 25 mmHg. How does arterial minus venous driving pressure change?

A. It falls by about 25 mmHg.

B. It rises by about 5 mmHg.

C. It falls by about 5 mmHg.

D. It rises by about 55 mmHg.

17. Redness spreads beyond a stroked patch of skin as the flare develops. What generates this spread?

A. Direct filtration through enlarged lymphatic vessels.

B. Venous thrombosis in the surrounding tissue.

C. Central spinal reflex contraction of the skin muscles.

D. Antidromic impulses in branches of a sensory axon.

18. A nurse notices ankle swelling after a long standing shift. Which immediate local change promotes this swelling?

A. Reduced capillary pressure decreases net filtration.

B. Lower interstitial pressure stops all capillary exchange.

C. Increased plasma oncotic pressure draws fluid outward.

D. Raised capillary pressure increases outward filtration.

19. A low-frequency third heart sound occurs in early diastole. What ventricular event coincides with it?

A. Atrial depolarisation before systole.

B. Isovolumetric ventricular contraction.

C. Rapid passive ventricular filling.

D. Opening of the aortic valve.

20. A small additional deflection appears after the T wave in an otherwise interpretable trace. Which description fits the U wave?

A. Atrial depolarisation occurring before the next P wave.

B. A possible late ventricular repolarisation phenomenon.

C. Rapid ventricular depolarisation immediately before QRS.

D. A ventricular plateau interval between QRS and T.

21. What is the main purpose of wide changes in skin blood flow during heating or cooling?

A. To maintain constant oxygen uptake by skin cells.

B. To alter the heart's intrinsic pacemaker rate.

C. To adjust body heat gain and loss.

D. To equalise pulmonary and systemic arterial pressures.

22. Why does sustained systemic hypertension tend to raise myocardial oxygen demand?

A. Aortic pressure removes myocardial wall tension.

B. The left ventricle stops contracting during systole.

C. Coronary venous oxygen content necessarily becomes arterial.

D. The ventricle must generate higher pressure for ejection.

23. Why is a distinct atrial repolarisation wave usually absent from a normal ECG?

D. It lasts 0.35 s and is longer than the stated usual range.

B. Flow of blood through competent venous valves.

C. Dilatation of cerebral vessels by carbon dioxide.

B. Compensatory dilation of brain vessels with increased driving pressure.

27. What closes the aortic valve at the beginning of ventricular relaxation?

B. Atrial pressure rises above left ventricular pressure.

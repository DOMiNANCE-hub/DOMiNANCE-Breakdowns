# Introduction to Circulation

## The heart is two pumps in series
The **right heart** receives blood from the systemic veins and pumps it through the pulmonary arteries to the lungs. Oxygenated blood returns by the pulmonary veins to the **left heart**, which pumps it to the systemic tissues. The same volume must pass through both circuits over time; otherwise blood accumulates in one circuit.

The circulation delivers oxygen, nutrients and hormones, removes wastes, and distributes heat. Arteries carry blood **away** from the heart, and veins carry it **towards** the heart: these names describe direction, not oxygen content. Thus, pulmonary arteries carry relatively deoxygenated blood.

## Pressure, flow and resistance
Blood travels because of a **pressure difference**. Over a vascular segment, flow can be represented as **Q = ΔP/R**, where **Q** is volume flow, **ΔP** the driving pressure difference and **R** resistance. This is a useful organising equation for much of CVS physiology.

The systemic circulation has relatively high resistance and pressure; the pulmonary circulation works at much lower pressure to protect the delicate lung capillaries. Both are supplied by roughly equal cardiac output.

## Electrical activity precedes pumping
Myocardial depolarization initiates contraction. **Systole** refers to contraction/ejection phases and **diastole** to relaxation/filling phases; exact timing depends on which chamber is being discussed.

## Follow one red blood cell through both circuits
Blood returning from systemic tissues enters the **right atrium** via the venae cavae, passes the **tricuspid valve** to the right ventricle, and travels through the **pulmonary valve** and pulmonary arteries to the lungs. Oxygenated blood returns in pulmonary veins to the **left atrium**, passes the **mitral valve** to the left ventricle, and leaves through the **aortic valve** into the systemic arteries. The pulmonary arteries carry relatively deoxygenated blood; the pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood. The names describe direction **relative to the heart**, not oxygen content.

Each circuit carries approximately the same mean output in steady state. The pulmonary circulation operates at **much lower pressures** because pulmonary resistance is low. The left ventricular myocardium is thicker because it pumps against high systemic vascular resistance.

## Why the valves do not need muscles to open
Heart valves open and close **passively in response to pressure differences**. When atrial pressure exceeds ventricular pressure during filling, the atrioventricular valves open. When ventricular pressure exceeds arterial pressure during ejection, semilunar valves open. Papillary muscles and chordae tendineae prevent AV leaflets from prolapsing during contraction; they do not pull the valve open.

**Practice:** If the pulmonary circuit suddenly developed abnormally high resistance, which ventricle would initially face a greater pressure workload? The **right ventricle**, because its afterload would rise.

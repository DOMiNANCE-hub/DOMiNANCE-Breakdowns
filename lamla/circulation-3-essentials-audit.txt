# DOMiNANCE LAMLA editorial audit — Circulation 3 / Lecture 5

**Scope:** The approved Chapter Essentials in `gravity.md`, `shock.md` and `heart-failure.md`; suggestions below are NOT applied to approved manuscripts. They identify shorthand that may confuse a student who has not read the chapter. Proposed replacements should be reviewed before any manuscript edit.

- Chapter: 19 — Gravity, Posture and Acceleration | Current line: "**Rescue:** baroreflex (faster, stronger heart; arterioles and veins constrict), **muscle pump**, then renin–angiotensin–aldosterone." | Suggested replacement: "**Rescue:** low pressure unloads baroreceptors → sympathetic output raises heart rate, contractility and vessel tone; leg-muscle pumping boosts return; RAAS retains sodium/water."
- Chapter: 19 — Gravity, Posture and Acceleration | Current line: "**The brain is protected** because venous pressure and ICP fall too, and local CO₂ dilates its arterioles." | Suggested replacement: "**Brain protection:** falling cerebral venous/ICP pressure preserves perfusion pressure; rising local CO₂ dilates cerebral arterioles, limiting the loss of flow."
- Chapter: 19 — Gravity, Posture and Acceleration | Current line: "**Vasovagal syncope:** sympathetic withdrawal + vagal activation → dilation and **bradycardia**. Also micturition and carotid sinus syncope." | Suggested replacement: "**Vasovagal syncope:** sympathetic withdrawal and vagal activation cause hypotension/bradycardia; urination or pressure on the carotid sinus can trigger other reflex faints."
- Chapter: 19 — Gravity, Posture and Acceleration | Current line: "**Anti-g suits** compress the abdomen and legs; **horizontal** positioning raises tolerance." | Suggested replacement: "**Anti-g suits** compress abdominal/leg veins to reduce pooling; a horizontal position shortens the vertical blood column, limiting pressure loss at the head."

- Chapter: 20 — Circulatory Shock | Current line: "**Four types:** hypovolaemic (volume), cardiogenic (pump), distributive (vessels dilate), obstructive (flow blocked)." | Suggested replacement: "**Four types:** hypovolaemic loses blood/fluid; cardiogenic has inadequate pumping; distributive dilates and pools blood; obstructive blocks cardiac filling or forward flow."
- Chapter: 20 — Circulatory Shock | Current line: "**Signs:** low BP; fast, weak pulse; pale, cool skin; low urine; rapid breathing; thirst; restless, then dull; lactic acidosis. Each has a mechanism." | Suggested replacement: "**Signs and causes:** low output lowers BP/pulse volume; baroreflex raises rate; skin/renal constriction causes pallor/oliguria; hypoxia causes tachypnoea/lactate; angiotensin II/ADH cause thirst; cerebral hypoperfusion causes apathy."
- Chapter: 20 — Circulatory Shock | Current line: "**Compensation:** baroreflex and chemoreceptors (seconds), adrenal medulla, renin–angiotensin–aldosterone, ADH, cortisol (minutes to hours), interstitial fluid moving into the blood (hours)." | Suggested replacement: "**Compensation:** nerves quickly raise cardiac/vascular drive; catecholamines reinforce it; RAAS retains salt, ADH retains water; low capillary pressure draws tissue fluid into plasma."
- Chapter: 20 — Circulatory Shock | Current line: "**Progressive shock:** heart fails, vasomotor centre fails, acidosis, capillary stasis and leak, kidney damage. **Irreversible shock:** no recovery even when volume is replaced." | Suggested replacement: "**Progressive shock:** ischaemia weakens heart and medullary drive; lactate blunts catecholamine responses; capillary stasis leaks fluid; kidneys fail; irreversible injury persists despite refilling."
- Chapter: 20 — Circulatory Shock | Current line: "**Distributive:** neurogenic, septic (warm skin early), anaphylactic (histamine). **Cardiogenic:** high venous pressure. **Obstructive:** tamponade, tension pneumothorax, pulmonary embolism." | Suggested replacement: "**Patterns:** neurogenic loses sympathetic tone; sepsis dilates/leaks; anaphylaxis releases histamine; cardiogenic failure backs blood into veins; tamponade, tension pneumothorax and embolism obstruct flow."

- Chapter: 21 — Heart Failure Physiology | Current line: "**Forward failure** = too little output. **Backward failure** = congestion upstream. They are two sides of one problem." | Suggested replacement: "**Forward failure** underperfuses organs because output is low; **backward failure** congests veins because the failing chamber cannot empty incoming blood."
- Chapter: 21 — Heart Failure Physiology | Current line: "**Rescue systems** (sympathetic, RAAS, ADH) hold up pressure at first, but raise afterload and fluid, so they worsen failure." | Suggested replacement: "**Rescue systems:** sympathetic nerves raise rate/vasoconstriction; angiotensin II raises afterload; aldosterone/ADH retain fluid → pressure briefly improves but congestion worsens."
- Chapter: 21 — Heart Failure Physiology | Current line: "**Paradox:** waterlogged, yet the kidneys sense \"too little blood\"." | Suggested replacement: "**Heart-failure paradox:** total body fluid rises, but poor cardiac output underfills arteries → kidneys retain more salt/water, worsening oedema."
- Chapter: 21 — Heart Failure Physiology | Current line: "**Frank–Starling** gives diminishing returns on a failing, flat curve." | Suggested replacement: "**Frank–Starling in failure:** added filling produces little extra stroke volume because the failing curve flattens, but pressure and congestion rise."

## Source differences and scope controls

- **Chapter 19:** The approved notes include specific orthostatic diagnostic criteria, a hydrostatic pressure gradient, about 500 mL of standing-related pooling, and a heart-rate increase. These refine slide shorthand; use approved figures.
- **Chapter 20:** The lecture's fourth-category list labels shock as vasogenic/low resistance, while the chapter uses the modern equivalent **distributive**; both refer to marked vasodilatation. The approved notes supply mechanism-oriented explanations for shock signs.
- **Chapter 21:** The slides chiefly teach forward/backward and left/right failure. The approved chapter additionally teaches systolic/diastolic failure and maladaptive sympathetic/RAAS/ADH responses; include them because the approved chapter is the source of truth.
- **Duplicate slides:** Circulation 3 and Lecture 5 cover the same Homeostasis material. ECG, CSF and coronary reference slides in Circulation 3 are not duplicated because dedicated approved chapters cover them.

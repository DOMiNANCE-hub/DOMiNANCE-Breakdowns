## Chapter Essentials: lines that need fuller wording

- Chapter: 6 — Electrocardiography and Basic Rhythms | Current line: "**The T wave is usually upright**, because ventricles reset in the reverse order to how they switched on." | Suggested replacement: "The T wave is usually upright when QRS is upright because ventricular repolarisation runs in the opposite direction and has the opposite electrical polarity."
- Chapter: 6 — Electrocardiography and Basic Rhythms | Current line: "**Leads:** I, II and III (bipolar, Einthoven's triangle, II = I + III); aVR, aVL and aVF; V1–V6 across the chest." | Suggested replacement: "I, II and III compare pairs of limb electrodes (II = I + III); aVR/aVL/aVF look from limbs; V1–V6 look across the chest."
- Chapter: 6 — Electrocardiography and Basic Rhythms | Current line: "**ST segment** = whole ventricle switched on (plateau): flat but active. It shifts in ischaemia." | Suggested replacement: "The ST segment is flat during the ventricular plateau despite contraction; myocardial ischaemia can elevate or depress it."
- Chapter: 6 — Electrocardiography and Basic Rhythms | Current line: "**Paper:** 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV. Small square = 0.04 s and 0.1 mV; large square = 0.20 s." | Suggested replacement: "At 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV, each small square is 0.04 s across or 0.1 mV up; each large square is 0.20 s or 0.5 mV."
- Chapter: 6 — Electrocardiography and Basic Rhythms | Current line: "**Heart block:** first degree = long PR, all conducted; second degree = some P waves dropped; third degree = P and QRS independent." | Suggested replacement: "First-degree block prolongs PR beyond 0.20 s without dropped QRS; second degree drops some QRS complexes; third degree disconnects P waves and QRS complexes."

## Source differences and scope notes

- Slide 12 includes the displayed Bazett relation `QTc = QT / √RR` and a figure caption giving `QTc ≤0.44 s`; Chapter 6 marks QT correction as beyond this lecture's core teaching. Retain this as editorial metadata rather than a required LAMLA memorisation target until approved.
- Slide 8 gives normal QRS duration as `0.06–0.10 s`; Chapter 6 also explains the conventional clinical normal cut-off of less than `0.12 s`. The sheet keeps the lecturer's range and treats clinical cut-off as supplementary.
- Chapter 6 adds lead positions, electrode/lead distinctions, rhythm assessment, electrical vector principles and AV-block patterns beyond the terse slide text; these are within the approved chapter scope.

# DOMiNANCE LAMLA editorial audit — Circulation 1: The Heart and Circulation

**Source:** The approved Chapter Essentials in Chapters 1–5 and 7–15. The proposed replacements below are editorial suggestions, NOT changes to the approved chapter manuscripts. They are specifically designed to avoid vague shorthand for readers starting from zero.

- Chapter: 1 — Introduction to the Heart and Circulation | Current line: "**Four chambers:** the atria receive blood and top up the ventricles; the ventricles generate the pressure." | Suggested replacement: "**Four chambers:** right atrium receives systemic venous blood, right ventricle pumps to lungs; left atrium receives pulmonary blood, left ventricle pumps to organs."
- Chapter: 1 — Introduction to the Heart and Circulation | Current line: "**Systole is contraction; diastole is relaxation.** The atria contract at the end of ventricular diastole. Electrical activation (SA node → AV node → His–Purkinje) comes first and sets the order of contraction." | Suggested replacement: "**Timing:** SA node activates atria first; AV delay permits ventricular filling; His–Purkinje activates ventricles next. Atrial systole occurs near the end of ventricular diastole."

- Chapter: 2 — Cardiac Muscle Physiology | Current line: "**The atria and ventricles are separate units.** Fibrous tissue insulates them, and the AV node is the only normal electrical route between them." | Suggested replacement: "**Two functional syncytia:** atrial and ventricular muscle are insulated by fibrous tissue, so impulses pass between them only through the normal AV conducting system."
- Chapter: 2 — Cardiac Muscle Physiology | Current line: "**Sympathetic (β₁ → cAMP → PKA):** stronger contraction and faster relaxation (phospholamban released). Xanthines raise cAMP by blocking its breakdown. Vagal stimulation slows the SA node and weakens the atria." | Suggested replacement: "**Autonomic control:** β₁/cAMP/PKA increases Ca²⁺ availability and SERCA activity, strengthening contraction and speeding relaxation; vagus slows SA rate; xanthines reduce cAMP breakdown."

- Chapter: 3 — Cardiac Conduction and Automaticity | Current line: "**Automaticity:** specialised cells fire on their own; the nerves only change the rate." | Suggested replacement: "**Automaticity:** pacemaker phase 4 depolarises spontaneously to threshold; sympathetic nerves steepen it, while vagal activity slows it."
- Chapter: 3 — Cardiac Conduction and Automaticity | Current line: "**Heart block:** first degree, late; second degree, some dropped; third degree, none get through, with the atria and ventricles beating independently." | Suggested replacement: "**Heart block:** first degree prolongs PR but conducts every P; second drops some P-to-QRS beats; third conducts none, so atria and ventricles run independently."

- Chapter: 4 — The Cardiac Cycle | Current line: "**One chain drives every phase:** electrical signal → contraction or relaxation → pressure change → valves open or close → blood moves and volume changes." | Suggested replacement: "**Cardiac sequence:** depolarisation triggers contraction and higher pressure; pressure crossings close/open valves; ejection reduces ventricular volume, while relaxation and AV reopening permit filling."
- Chapter: 4 — The Cardiac Cycle | Current line: "**On a Wiggers diagram, every valve event is a point where two pressure curves cross.**" | Suggested replacement: "**Wiggers valve events:** AV valves close when ventricular exceeds atrial pressure; semilunar valves open when ventricular exceeds arterial pressure; the reverse gradients close/open them."
- Chapter: 4 — The Cardiac Cycle | Current line: "**Atrial pressure waves:** a = atria contract; c = shut AV valves bulge back; x = atria relax and the valve ring is pulled down; v = atria fill behind shut valves; y = AV valves open and the atria empty." | Suggested replacement: "**Atrial waves:** a = atrial contraction; c = valve bulging; x = atrial relaxation/annular descent; v = venous filling behind shut AV valve; y = AV opening/atrial emptying."

- Chapter: 5 — Heart Sounds and Murmurs | Current line: "**Loudness**: a slow heart gives a soft S1 (the cusps have floated nearly shut). High aortic or pulmonary pressure gives a loud A2 or P2. Loudness alone does not measure severity." | Suggested replacement: "**Sound loudness:** slow rate lets AV leaflets drift toward closure, softening S1; high aortic/pulmonary pressure strengthens A2/P2, but loudness is not a direct severity scale."
- Chapter: 5 — Heart Sounds and Murmurs | Current line: "**Bruits** follow the same rule as murmurs: find the pressure difference driving the abnormal flow, and when it exists. A PDA is continuous because aortic pressure stays above pulmonary pressure all the time." | Suggested replacement: "**Bruits:** narrowed/abnormally connected vessels generate turbulent flow; a PDA produces continuous flow because aortic pressure exceeds pulmonary pressure in systole and diastole."

- Chapter: 7 — Cardiac Output and the Frank–Starling Law | Current line: "**Along the curve** = preload change (heterometric). **To a new curve** = contractility change (homometric). Afterload also shifts curves." | Suggested replacement: "**Starling graph:** more preload moves the heart along one curve (heterometric); altered intrinsic contractility moves it between curves (homometric), changing output at the same EDV."
- Chapter: 7 — Cardiac Output and the Frank–Starling Law | Current line: "**EDV rises** with more blood volume, venous tone, muscle and breathing pumps, atrial contraction and longer filling. **It falls** with blood loss, standing, tamponade, stiff ventricles and very fast rates." | Suggested replacement: "**EDV:** better blood volume/venous return/atrial filling raises preload; blood loss, venous pooling, tamponade, stiffness or short diastole restrict filling and lower effective EDV."
- Chapter: 7 — Cardiac Output and the Frank–Starling Law | Current line: "**Exercise** raises HR, contractility and venous return together. Blood loss lowers preload, and tachycardia may not save CO." | Suggested replacement: "**Output responses:** exercise activates sympathetic rate/inotropy and muscle/respiratory venous pumps; haemorrhage reduces preload, so even compensatory tachycardia may fail to maintain output."

- Chapter: 8 — Measuring Cardiac Output and the Work of the Heart | Current line: "**Use content, not saturation**: content depends on haemoglobin." | Suggested replacement: "**Fick oxygen measure:** use mL O₂ per mL blood, not saturation percentage alone, because anaemia changes content even at unchanged saturation."
- Chapter: 8 — Measuring Cardiac Output and the Work of the Heart | Current line: "**Samples:** arterial from any artery; **mixed venous from the pulmonary artery**." | Suggested replacement: "**Fick sampling:** systemic arterial blood represents incoming oxygen content; pulmonary arterial blood mixes venous return from the body to represent extracted oxygen."
- Chapter: 8 — Measuring Cardiac Output and the Work of the Heart | Current line: "**Heart muscle oxygen demand** rises with wall tension, contractility and heart rate. **Pressure work costs more than volume work.**" | Suggested replacement: "**Oxygen demand:** faster, stronger beats and high wall pressure raise ATP use; pressure loading markedly increases ventricular wall tension, often costing more oxygen than volume loading."

- Chapter: 9 — Blood Flow and Haemodynamics | Current line: "**Vessel roles:** elastic arteries (recoil), arterioles (resistance), capillaries (exchange), veins (capacity)." | Suggested replacement: "**Vessel roles:** arteries recoil to sustain diastolic flow; arterioles vary radius to control resistance; capillaries exchange substances; compliant veins store blood."
- Chapter: 9 — Blood Flow and Haemodynamics | Current line: "**Viscosity** rises with haematocrit. Polycythaemia and anaemia both raise cardiac work, by different routes." | Suggested replacement: "**Haematocrit effects:** polycythaemia thickens blood and raises resistance; anaemia lowers oxygen content, increasing compensatory output and cardiac work despite lower viscosity."
- Chapter: 9 — Blood Flow and Haemodynamics | Current line: "**Doppler** measures velocity; flow = velocity × area." | Suggested replacement: "**Doppler flow:** measured velocity alone is not volume flow; multiply by the vessel's cross-sectional area to calculate blood volume per unit time."

- Chapter: 10 — Arterial Blood Pressure | Current line: "**MAP ≈ CO × SVR.** BP depends on output and resistance, not heart rate alone. **Normal BP ≠ normal flow.**" | Suggested replacement: "**Mean pressure:** CO × vascular resistance sets MAP; arteriolar constriction can maintain near-normal pressure during haemorrhage despite falling output and organ perfusion."
- Chapter: 10 — Arterial Blood Pressure | Current line: "**The taps** come from blood forcing through the squeezed brachial artery, not from the heart valves." | Suggested replacement: "**Korotkoff taps:** cuff deflation below systolic allows intermittent turbulent jets through compressed brachial artery; sounds disappear near diastolic as flow becomes less disturbed."

- Chapter: 11 — Arterial Pulse and Jugular Venous Pulse | Current line: "**Pulse features (lecture):** rate, volume, strength, character and location, plus rhythm. A pulse deficit means some beats are too weak to reach the wrist." | Suggested replacement: "**Pulse assessment:** rate counts waves; rhythm checks spacing; volume/strength/character describe wave force and shape; pulse deficit means some ventricular beats are not palpable peripherally."
- Chapter: 11 — Arterial Pulse and Jugular Venous Pulse | Current line: "**The JVP shows right atrial pressure**, because no valve separates the right atrium from the neck veins. It can be seen but not felt, and it falls on inspiration." | Suggested replacement: "**JVP:** direct venous connection transmits right atrial pressure to neck; low-pressure waves are visible but not palpable, and inspiration lowers thoracic venous pressure."

- Chapter: 12 — Veins and Venous Return | Current line: "**No filling, no output:** even a strong heart can't pump blood it doesn't receive." | Suggested replacement: "**Venous return determines preload:** reduced return lowers EDV → Frank–Starling stroke volume and output fall even when intrinsic contractility remains strong."
- Chapter: 12 — Veins and Venous Return | Current line: "**Venoconstriction** squeezes the reservoir and raises venous return without adding blood." | Suggested replacement: "**Venoconstriction:** sympathetic narrowing reduces venous capacitance → stored blood shifts centrally → mean filling pressure and venous return rise without extra total volume."

- Chapter: 13 — Microcirculation and Capillary Exchange | Current line: "**Three types:** continuous (tightest in the brain), fenestrated (gut, glands, kidney), sinusoidal (liver, spleen, marrow)." | Suggested replacement: "**Capillary types:** continuous endothelium restricts exchange, fenestrations speed fluid/solute transfer, and discontinuous sinusoids allow large substances to cross more freely."
- Chapter: 13 — Microcirculation and Capillary Exchange | Current line: "**Arterioles and precapillary sphincters** control which capillaries get blood. Active tissue opens more." | Suggested replacement: "**Capillary recruitment:** local tissue metabolites dilate upstream arterioles and relax precapillary sphincters → more microvascular paths receive blood during activity."
- Chapter: 13 — Microcirculation and Capillary Exchange | Current line: "**Starling tug-of-war:** Pc pushes out, πc (albumin) pulls in; the tissue forces Pi and πi are smaller." | Suggested replacement: "**Starling forces:** capillary pressure Pc promotes outward filtration; plasma albumin oncotic πc opposes it; tissue hydrostatic Pi and oncotic πi modify net water movement."

- Chapter: 14 — Formation of Oedema | Current line: "**Five routes:** push too high (Pc ↑), pull too weak (albumin ↓), wall too leaky, drain blocked, or too much fluid overall." | Suggested replacement: "**Five routes to oedema:** raised capillary pressure, reduced plasma albumin, increased endothelial permeability, blocked lymph return or renal fluid retention raise tissue fluid accumulation."
- Chapter: 14 — Formation of Oedema | Current line: "**Ask where the swelling is**: it points to the mechanism that failed." | Suggested replacement: "**Oedema distribution:** bilateral dependent swelling suggests systemic venous/volume causes; one swollen limb suggests local blockage; pulmonary fluid suggests left-sided heart failure."

- Chapter: 15 — Lymphatic Circulation | Current line: "**Lymph moves** by vessel-wall contractions, valves, the muscle pump, breathing and pulsing arteries. There is no heart, and nodes don't pump." | Suggested replacement: "**Lymph propulsion:** intrinsic lymphatic contractions and external muscle, respiratory and arterial compression propel lymph proximally; one-way valves prevent reflux, nodes filter rather than pump."
- Chapter: 15 — Lymphatic Circulation | Current line: "**Lymph flow can rise several-fold** to prevent oedema. Swelling appears when filtration outstrips it or drainage fails." | Suggested replacement: "**Lymphatic reserve:** increased filtration raises lymph formation and collecting-vessel pumping; persistent swelling develops once lymphatic transport capacity is exceeded or obstructed."
- Chapter: 15 — Lymphatic Circulation | Current line: "**Lost lymphatics** → swelling, weaker immune defence, poor fat absorption." | Suggested replacement: "**Lymphatic loss:** trapped tissue fluid/protein causes swelling; lost node transport impairs immune surveillance; failed intestinal lacteal drainage reduces long-chain fat absorption."

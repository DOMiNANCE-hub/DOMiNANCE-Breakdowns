# Neural and Hormonal Cardiovascular Regulation

## The short-term neural controller
Brainstem cardiovascular networks (including medullary nuclei) coordinate sympathetic and parasympathetic output. Sympathetic nerves release mainly **noradrenaline** from postganglionic endings. **α₁ receptors** on many systemic resistance vessels cause vasoconstriction; sympathetic activation also constricts many veins, reducing capacitance. The heart responds mainly via **β₁ receptors**, increasing heart rate, conduction and contractility.

Vagal **acetylcholine–M₂** signalling slows SA nodal rate and AV conduction. The vagus normally exerts considerable resting restraint on heart rate, so atropine or vagal withdrawal may raise it.

## Why BP depends on two linked factors
Approximately **MAP ≈ CO × SVR** if right atrial pressure is small. Cardiac sympathetic effects often increase CO; arteriolar α-mediated constriction increases systemic vascular resistance. Venoconstriction can also support preload, allowing output to rise when circulating volume is adequate.

## Humoral regulation
- **Angiotensin II** strongly constricts vessels and promotes aldosterone release.
- **Aldosterone** helps retain sodium and water over hours/days.
- **Vasopressin (ADH)** promotes renal water retention; at higher concentrations it can constrict vessels.
- **Adrenaline** acts on different α and β receptors depending on dose and vascular bed.
- **Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP)** promotes natriuresis and tends to counter volume expansion.
- Endothelium-derived NO, endothelin and local autacoids modify regional tone.

## Time scale is important
Neural regulation can act in seconds. Renal sodium/water balance and RAAS contribute importantly over longer intervals. Both levels interact rather than operating independently.

**Check:** Why can sympathetic activation raise BP through vessels even if HR changes little?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Arteriolar vasoconstriction raises systemic vascular resistance, while venoconstriction may support preload. Both influence arterial pressure independently of a large heart-rate change.</details>

## Trace the medullary cardiovascular network
The lecturer distinguishes medullary **pressor** and **depressor** regions. Modern functional descriptions identify the **nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS)** as a major sensory integrator, the **rostral ventrolateral medulla (RVLM)** as an important source of sympathetic vasomotor drive, and the **caudal ventrolateral medulla (CVLM)** as an inhibitory relay. These networks alter sympathetic and vagal traffic to heart and vessels.

**Tonic sympathetic vasoconstrictor discharge** maintains baseline arteriolar and venous tone. When discharge increases, **α₁ receptors** generally increase vascular smooth-muscle contraction. Decreasing tonic drive allows vasodilatation; it does not require a separate continuously active vasodilator nerve. Some vascular beds have specialised autonomic dilator influences, but the central teaching is widespread sympathetic vasoconstrictor tone and locally regulated perfusion.

## Separate vascular tone from heart rate
**β₁** stimulation of SA node/ventricular myocardium can increase heart rate and contractility. **M₂** vagal signalling reduces sinus rate and AV conduction. Because **CO = HR × SV**, an increased HR does not guarantee increased CO when reduced filling lowers SV. Because **MAP ≈ CO × SVR** (when right atrial pressure is small), even a falling CO may coexist with temporarily maintained MAP when SVR increases.

## Choose the correct time scale
Neural baroreflex responses act within **seconds**. **Angiotensin II** and catecholamines influence vascular tone over seconds to minutes; **aldosterone**, renal pressure natriuresis and sodium balance influence blood volume over hours to days. **ANP** is released with atrial stretch and promotes renal salt/water excretion. **ADH** supports water retention and at high concentrations can contribute to vasoconstriction.

**Practice:** A person receives atropine, blocking M₂ receptors. Resting HR often **rises** because tonic vagal restraint of the SA node is reduced.

# Formation of Oedema

## Oedema is excessive interstitial fluid
Interstitial fluid volume depends on fluid leaving the microvessels, its return through lymphatics, and tissue properties. Oedema results when filtration exceeds lymphatic drainage capacity, or when drainage is impaired.

## Main mechanisms
1. **Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure** — e.g. venous obstruction, prolonged standing, some forms of heart failure.
2. **Reduced plasma oncotic pressure** — e.g. marked hypoalbuminaemia from liver disease, nephrotic protein loss or severe malnutrition.
3. **Increased endothelial permeability** — e.g. inflammation, burns, allergic mediator release.
4. **Lymphatic obstruction/dysfunction** — e.g. lymph node removal, tumour, filariasis.
5. **Renal sodium/water retention** — expands extracellular fluid and can increase microvascular hydrostatic pressures.

## Why location matters
Dependent ankle oedema increases after prolonged standing because gravitational hydrostatic pressure rises in leg veins. Pulmonary oedema can occur when elevated pulmonary venous pressure drives excess filtration into lung interstitium and alveoli, disrupting gas exchange.

Pitting oedema usually reflects free interstitial fluid; established lymphoedema may become less pitting due to fibrosis. The distinction is useful but not absolute.

## Put Starling's forces into one equation
A useful traditional expression is **Jv = Kf[(Pc − Pi) − σ(πc − πi)]**, where Jv is net fluid movement, Kf filtration coefficient, Pc/Pi hydrostatic pressures inside capillary/interstitium, πc/πi their colloid osmotic pressures, and σ the protein reflection coefficient. Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure (**venous obstruction or heart failure**), reduced plasma oncotic pressure (**hypoalbuminaemia**), increased barrier permeability (**inflammation**) or obstructed lymphatics can all produce interstitial fluid accumulation.

The lecturer's **venous-end reabsorption diagram (Circulation 1, slide 87)** represents a classic teaching model. The modern **endothelial glycocalyx** model places the main relevant oncotic gradient across the glycocalyx and recognises that many continuous capillaries have persistent small net filtration at steady state, with filtered fluid returned by **lymphatics**. Transient reabsorption can still occur after a fall in capillary hydrostatic pressure; the old universal '85% reabsorbed at venous end' formulation is **not a universal rule**.

## Predict the distribution of oedema
Raised systemic venous pressure causes dependent oedema, often most evident at ankles while upright. Left heart failure can raise **pulmonary venous pressure**, causing pulmonary congestion/oedema. Hypoalbuminaemia lowers the force retaining fluid intravascularly and can produce more widespread oedema. Lymphatic obstruction often produces chronic local swelling with tissue remodelling and may become relatively **non-pitting**, while early lymphoedema can still pit.

**Practice:** How can prolonged standing cause ankle swelling? Gravity increases dependent venous/capillary hydrostatic pressure, raising filtration; without sufficient muscle/lymph pumping, fluid accumulates.
